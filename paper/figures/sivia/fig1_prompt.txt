CREATE ONE PUBLICATION-STYLE SCIENTIFIC ILLUSTRATION.

SOURCE CONTRACT
Paper: Who Contributes What in Human-AI Research? A Qualitative Case Study of a Project That Analyses Itself (submission to the ICIS 2026 workshop on Autonomous Scholarship).
Source of facts: the manuscript text, the frozen coding manual (version 1.1) and the recorded project sessions. Nothing in the figure is a measured result. All small charts, matrices and cases are schematic.
Figure claim: Recorded sessions of a human-AI research project are cut into units, each unit is tagged by independent coders with an actor and one of four cognitive contribution types, and the tagged units answer three qualitative questions about existence, distribution and transition, while each manuscript revision feeds new sessions back into the record.
Role of this figure: method overview for the whole paper, printed across the full text width of a conference paper.

DENSITY GATE, HIGHEST PRIORITY
Use a 3:2 landscape canvas on a white background. Keep the meaningful content within 2% to 98% of the width and 3% to 97% of the height. Fill 85% to 92% of the canvas width and 80% to 92% of its height with meaningful scientific objects. There is no empty banner, no empty lower third, no title, no title strip and no decorative margin. The first panel headers start at the very top of the content area, so the full height is used for scenes. Enlarge scenes and objects until the largest empty rectangle is smaller than 3% of the canvas area, keeping only narrow connector lanes. Every large light area contains a scene, a representation or a necessary connector lane.

EXACT VISIBLE LABELS
Only these short labels appear in the artwork, and they are spelled exactly as written. The figure has no title, because its caption carries the title. Region headers: Recorded project, Units, Tags, Existence, Distribution, Transition. Actor names: Human, Primary agent, Sub-agents. Unit labels: One response per unit, Tool steps as evidence. Coder labels: Coder A, Coder B, Agreement. Type labels: Knowledge, Proposal, Decision, Metacognition, Execution. Short type definitions: a fact or concept enters, a candidate is offered, the project is committed, the work is checked or steered, a step is carried out. Rule labels: Product changes, Only the process changes. Analysis labels: RQ1 Existence, RQ2 Distribution, RQ3 Transition, Mixed contribution, Interaction modes, schematic. Loop label: Each revision adds sessions and the coding is repeated. Production prose is never typeset.

CANVAS AND COMPOSITION
Compose the canvas as two bands and a thin return lane. The upper band occupies about 58% of the height and holds three scenes in a left-to-right reading spine. The lower band occupies about 37% of the height and holds three scenes side by side. A thin return lane occupies the remaining bottom 5% and runs along the lower edge. The upper scenes have unequal widths because their content differs. The first scene, Recorded project, takes about 22% of the width. The second scene, Units, takes about 25%. The third scene, Tags, takes about 53% and is the focal region of the whole figure with the largest objects and the strongest saturation. The lower scenes Existence, Distribution and Transition each take about one third of the width.

Reading order. The eye enters at the top left with the three actors and their recorded conversation, follows the continuous arrow to the cut units, continues into the large Tags scene where coders apply the four types, and then drops through one broad downward arrow into the three analyses along the lower band. The return lane closes the loop from the lower right back to the top left.

Region headers are narrow bold bands with white text on a blue-grey fill, each one about 5% of the height, with pill ends. The three upper headers use progressively deeper blue-grey fills to suggest progression. The three lower headers use the same deep blue-grey as the Tags header. Every scene sits in a very light panel with a thin dashed grey outline and a small corner radius, with narrow gutters of about 1% of the width.

RECORDED PROJECT SCENE
Place the header Recorded project at the top of the first panel. Under it, arrange a vertical conversation timeline that occupies most of the panel. On the left side of the timeline draw three small actor figures stacked from top to bottom with a short label under each. First a friendly researcher shown from the chest up with short dark hair, round glasses and a light blue shirt, labeled Human. Second a rounded white and light blue robot with a round head, two eyes, a small antenna and one raised hand, labeled Primary agent. Third two smaller rounded robots standing side by side with teal and light blue bodies, labeled Sub-agents.

To the right of the actors draw one continuous vertical transcript lane made of speech bubbles in time order from top to bottom. Draw six bubbles. The first is a human bubble with a small researcher face icon at its left edge. The second and third are primary agent bubbles with a small robot face icon. Between the second and third bubbles place a row of four small grey gear chips to show tool steps. The fourth is a human bubble. The fifth is a primary agent bubble preceded by three grey gear chips. The sixth is a small bubble with a sub-agent icon, attached to the fifth by a short thin branch line to show that a sub-agent session is spawned. Each bubble contains two or three abstract grey text lines and no letters. Human bubbles have a pale blue fill and a navy outline. AI bubbles have a pale grey fill and a dark slate outline. Tool step chips are small rounded grey squares with a tiny gear glyph.

Below the transcript place one short caption line at the bottom of the panel that contains only small icons and no sentence: a small stack of paper pages with a speech bubble and a small tag-free export arrow. This conveys that every session is exported. Do not add words to this icon row.

A broad blue-grey arrow leaves the right edge of this panel at the vertical middle and points into the Units panel. The arrow has a solid triangular head, a thick shaft and clear space around it.

UNITS SCENE
Place the header Units at the top of the second panel. The scene shows the same transcript cut into units, so that the reader sees the operation of cutting and merging. Draw four unit groups stacked vertically with equal horizontal alignment. Each unit group is a rounded outline with a light fill and a small icon at its left edge.

Unit one is a human unit. It contains one human bubble only and has a small researcher icon. It carries no gear chips. Unit two is a primary agent unit. It contains a row of three grey gear chips at the left, a small curved arrow that merges these chips into the following bubble, and one primary agent bubble at the right. The merge arrow is thin and dark and points from the chips into the bubble. The row of chips carries the small label Tool steps as evidence. Unit three is a primary agent unit with two gear chips merged into one bubble. Unit four is a pair of sub-agent units, drawn as two smaller nested outlines side by side with a small teal robot icon, connected to unit three by a short dashed spawn line with a tiny branch junction at unit three.

Use a thick bracket on the left side of the second panel that spans the four groups and ends with the label One response per unit. Do not draw numbers. The bracket is a clean right-facing curly brace in dark slate. Under the four groups place a short dotted line with a small scissors icon at the left to indicate the cutting operation. The scissors icon is small and flat. Leave about 5% of the panel height at the bottom for this strip.

A broad blue-grey arrow leaves the right edge of the Units panel and enters the large Tags panel at its left edge, aligned with the arrow from the first panel so that the three upper scenes read as one continuous spine.

TAGS SCENE, FOCAL REGION
Place the header Tags at the top of the large third panel. Divide the panel into three sub-scenes arranged left to right, with unequal widths of about 30%, 34% and 36%, each separated by a thin vertical dotted divider. The strongest visual mass of the whole figure is here.

Left sub-scene, one unit under review. Draw a single large unit card in the left sub-scene. The card is a rounded rectangle with a light fill, a navy outline and a small researcher face icon at its upper left corner. Inside the card draw a large human speech bubble with three abstract grey text lines and one short orange-tinted highlight bar over the second line. Under the bubble draw a short row of grey gear chips to show tool steps are part of the evidence. Below the card draw two thin straight lines that split towards the two coders, so the same card is read by both coders.

Middle sub-scene, two independent coders. Draw two rounded robot heads with headphones, one at the top labeled Coder A and one at the bottom labeled Coder B. They differ slightly in shape and colour: Coder A has a white head with light blue ear pieces, Coder B has a white head with teal ear pieces. Each robot holds a small open booklet that represents the coding manual. Next to each coder draw a short horizontal row of two small rounded tag chips that carry the actor and the type. The tag chips of Coder A show a small researcher icon followed by an orange chip with a signpost glyph, and a small robot icon followed by a green chip with a blueprint glyph. The tag chips of Coder B show the same two pairs, with one difference: the second chip pair of Coder B is purple instead of green, to show that coders sometimes assign different types. A small double-headed vertical arrow in the middle connects the two chip rows and ends in a small balance glyph with the label Agreement. A short grey bar below it with a tick mark shows that agreement is computed. Do not print percentages.

Right sub-scene, four contribution types and the boundary rule. Draw four large tiles in a two by two grid, each tile with a light fill, a deeper outline of the same hue, a bold short type label at the top, a detailed glyph in the centre and a one-line definition at the bottom. Top left tile, Knowledge, blue (fill #E3ECF5, outline #4E79A7), glyph an open book resting on two closed books with a small green bookmark ribbon, definition a fact or concept enters. Top right tile, Proposal, green (fill #E5F1E3, outline #59A14F), glyph a rolled blueprint with a sketched diagram of three boxes and lines plus a pencil and a green triangular set square, definition a candidate is offered. Bottom left tile, Decision, orange (fill #FCE9D6, outline #F28E2B), glyph a wooden signpost with two board signs pointing in opposite directions and a round green check mark on the upper board, definition the project is committed. Bottom right tile, Metacognition, purple (fill #EBDDEA, outline #9B6A8E), glyph a clipboard with three tick boxes and a magnifying glass held over it with a small teal gear, definition the work is checked or steered.

Below the four tiles draw a slim wide grey tile labeled Execution with a gear and wrench glyph on its left and the short definition a step is carried out. This tile is clearly subordinate: it is flatter, has a grey fill and a grey outline, and takes about one fifth of the height of the right sub-scene.

Below the grey tile draw the boundary rule as a small fork. A small rounded question tag at the left carries a tiny magnifying glass over a document icon. Two short arrows leave it. The upper arrow, labeled Product changes, leads to a small bracket that covers the three tiles Knowledge, Proposal and Decision. The lower arrow, labeled Only the process changes, leads to a small arrow into the Metacognition tile. Keep the fork compact so that it does not compete with the tiles, and keep both arrows short and orthogonal.

At the right edge of the Tags panel, below the tiles, draw a compact output cluster: two tag chips joined by a small plus sign, one with a researcher icon and the orange signpost glyph, one with a robot icon and the green blueprint glyph. This is the result of tagging one pair of units. It sits at the bottom right and connects to the lower band through a broad downward arrow.

BROAD DOWNWARD ARROW
A single broad blue-grey arrow leaves the bottom edge of the Tags panel near its centre and points down into the lower band. It divides into three short branches just above the lower band: one to each of the three lower panels. Use clean orthogonal routing with small rounded corners. The branches end with solid arrowheads above the header of each lower panel and do not touch any label.

EXISTENCE SCENE
Place the header Existence at the top of the lower left panel. The scene is a matrix drawn as a small grid, with three rows for the actors Human, Primary agent and Sub-agents, each introduced by its small icon, and four columns for the four types, each introduced by its small type glyph in the type colour. Each cell is a rounded square with a thin grey outline. A cell that shows presence contains a small filled circle in the colour of its type. A cell that shows absence is empty. Fill the grid with a schematic pattern in which most cells of the first two rows are filled and the third row has a few filled and a few empty cells, and place the small label schematic in grey italic at the lower right of the grid. Do not print numbers.

To the right of the grid draw a compact mixed contribution scene. Two small cards, one with a researcher icon and a blue knowledge book glyph, one with a robot icon and a green blueprint glyph, point with thin arrows into one common small document labeled Mixed contribution. Two small interlocking gears, one light blue and one orange, sit on the document corner to indicate that both actors shaped the same output. Under it draw a small crossed-out bubble with the word OK removed and only three dots left, to show that a bare approval does not count; draw it grey and small, without any words. Keep the whole mixed scene in the right third of the panel and the grid in the left two thirds.

DISTRIBUTION SCENE
Place the header Distribution at the top of the lower middle panel. The scene has two stacked representations. The upper representation is a small grouped bar chart with three groups of four bars, one group for each actor with its icon under the baseline, and four bars in the four type colours blue, green, orange and purple with different heights. The bars have thin dark outlines, a thin baseline and no axis numbers. Put the small label schematic in grey italic at the top right of the chart. The lower representation is a horizontal strip of nine small rounded stage chips in two rows, each chip with a single tiny glyph and no text: a magnifying glass for literature, a lightbulb for theory, a pencil for design, a flask for analysis, a speech bubble for interpretation, a document for writing, a checklist for verification, a circular arrow for revision and a gear for tooling. Colour all chips a neutral light grey with dark outlines. A thin bracket from the chart to the strip shows that the same tags are also arranged by research stage.

TRANSITION SCENE
Place the header Transition at the top of the lower right panel. The scene shows a small set of ordered tag chips joined by arrows, so that the reader sees how contributions pass between the actors. Draw two horizontal lanes, a human lane on top with a small researcher icon at its left edge and an AI lane at the bottom with a small robot icon at its left edge. Draw five chips along the two lanes in zigzag order, each with a small type glyph and a type colour: an orange Decision chip in the human lane, a green Proposal chip in the AI lane, an orange Decision chip in the human lane, a blue Knowledge chip in the AI lane, a purple Metacognition chip in the human lane. Arrows run from each chip to the next, crossing between the lanes, so that a zigzag path emerges. A small curved arrow from the last human chip back to the first chip shows a loop.

At the right edge of the panel draw a small label Interaction modes with a miniature legend of three small glyphs: a loop arrow for exchange, a branch arrow for supply, and a straight arrow for regulation. These glyphs have no text. Keep this legend small and aligned at the right edge of the panel. Put the small label schematic in grey italic at the bottom left of the lanes.

RETURN LANE
Along the bottom edge of the canvas draw a thin return lane of dark blue-grey colour. It begins at the lower right of the Transition panel with a small document icon with a pencil, which stands for the manuscript, and no arrowhead. It runs left under all three lower panels, rises along the left canvas edge up to the first panel and ends with exactly one solid arrowhead where it enters the Recorded project panel from the lower left. A small stack of pages icon sits next to that entry to indicate the added sessions. The lane has no arrowhead anywhere else, in particular none at its lower right start. Print the label Each revision adds sessions and the coding is repeated in small dark text inside a white pill that sits on the lane at the bottom centre. Keep the lane narrow and well clear of every other label.

TITLE
The figure has no title and no title strip. The caption in the paper provides the title. Do not print any title text.

CONNECTORS AND EDGE SEMANTICS
Primary data flow uses broad blue-grey arrows with solid triangular heads. Association lines use thin dotted grey lines without arrowheads. The spawn line uses a thin dashed dark line with a small junction dot at the actual branch point. The return lane is a dark continuous line and marks feedback. Arrow routes are orthogonal with a small radius at the corners. No line passes behind a label or through an icon. Every arrowhead touches a boundary without covering text. Do not draw junction dots at incidental crossings. In the lower band, branches from the downward arrow end above each panel and never run through a header.

VISUAL GRAMMAR AND COLOUR
The palette is a restrained set of blue-grey for structure, navy for text and four semantic type colours that keep a stable meaning throughout the figure: blue for Knowledge, green for Proposal, orange for Decision, purple for Metacognition, grey for Execution. Actors are never expressed through colour. They are always expressed through a researcher face icon or a robot face icon, so that no actor can be confused with a type. White fills are used inside tiles and bubbles. Use a flat vector illustration style with a thin uniform dark navy outline on every icon, flat fills with one soft shadow tone and light from the upper left, no gradients on the background and no photographs. Keep icons consistent in outline weight and level of detail, as if drawn by one illustrator. The figure must still read in grayscale, since structure comes from headers, outlines, arrows and the position of the large Tags panel.

TYPOGRAPHY
Use a clean sans-serif typeface. Use one bold weight for the region headers and one regular weight for labels. Region headers about 2.8% of the height. Type labels about 2.2% of the height. Small labels such as Coder A, Tool steps as evidence and One response per unit about 1.7% of the height. The smallest label is never smaller than 1.5% of the canvas height, which stays readable when the figure is printed at the full text width of a page. Use sentence case and avoid all capital banners.

WHAT EACH SCENE MUST COMMUNICATE
Recorded project: three kinds of actors produce one chronological record that includes tool steps. Units: the record is cut so that a response and the tool steps that prepare it become one unit and a human message is one unit; sub-agent sessions branch from the unit that spawned them. Tags: independent coders read the same unit with the same manual and assign actor-type tags, and the four types differ by what the move does in the shared record. Existence: the tagged units show which actors contribute which types and where two actors shape one output. Distribution: the same tags are summarised by type and by research stage as a reference. Transition: consecutive tagged units form exchanges between the actors, which are grouped into interaction modes. Return lane: the project that produces the manuscript is also the data, so the analysis repeats after every revision.

ATOMIC ASSETS AND LATER RECONSTRUCTION
Draw each icon as a separate, clearly outlined object with a clean silhouette on its own, because the icons will be generated again as independent transparent assets and inserted into an editable diagram. Keep labels, arrows, brackets, panels and headers as clean geometric elements that can be rebuilt as native diagram objects. Do not paint chart axes, numbers or text lines as realistic data. Charts and matrices are schematic and carry the small label schematic.

WHAT THE FIGURE MUST NOT SHOW
Do not print percentages, counts, kappa values, model names or any result. Do not add a legend box, a status board, a disclaimer, a production note or a list of steps. Do not print a title. Do not use magenta or pink anywhere. Do not draw photographs. Do not place any label that is not listed above. Do not create a card wall of equal rounded rectangles: the Tags panel must be visibly the largest and most saturated scene, the three upper scenes must differ in width, and the lower scenes must contain diagrams rather than text.

FINAL APPEARANCE
Render one complete visual draft that matches this instantiated specification. The picture should communicate the pipeline and the four types before the reader reads any label. The result looks like a carefully composed scientific method illustration with lively but restrained icons, not a glossy product diagram and not a poster full of text. Keep scientific fields atomic in appearance, with separate frames, symbols and connectors that are suitable for later editable reconstruction. Do not print section headings of this specification, editing instructions, provenance notes or approval requests inside the image. The final artwork contains the scientific scene and its concise labels only.
