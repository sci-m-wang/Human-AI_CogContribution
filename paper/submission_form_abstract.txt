Abstract (for the OpenReview submission form, not for the paper file)

Disclosure of AI use in research usually lists tasks, which shows activity and hides the thinking that each party contributes. This paper asks how humans and AI share the thinking when they do research together. It builds a typology of observable cognitive contributions, namely Knowledge, Proposal, Decision and Metacognition, together with a trace-based coding standard. The typology is applied to the project that produced this paper, in which a human orchestrator directed AI agents and every session was recorded. Two LLM coders from different model families coded 265 units. Human, AI and mixed contributions all exist. The human committed to the direction of the work, the AI supplied knowledge, proposals and verification, and seven outputs carry explicit contributions from both. Reading 38 delegation episodes against the codes yields an account of delegation and commitment with three propositions on bounded delegation, retained commitment and knowledge-induced reframing. The typology and the propositions extend IS work on delegation to AI and give disclosure a vocabulary beyond task lists.

Keywords: Human-AI collaboration, Delegation, Cognitive contribution, Autonomous scholarship, Generative AI, Qualitative case study
